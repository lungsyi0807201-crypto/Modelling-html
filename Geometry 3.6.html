DSE M2 Standard Derivation

Volume of a Right Circular Cone

Graphical Setup:

Consider revolving the straight line passing through the origin $(0,0)$ and $(h, r)$ about the $x$-axis from $x = 0$ to $x = h$.

Step 1: Write equation & isolate $y^2$

The straight line has gradient $m = \frac{r}{h}$. The equation is:

$$y = \frac{r}{h}x \implies \mathbf{y^2 = \frac{r^2}{h^2}x^2}$$
Step 2: Setup integration limits

The cone starts at the vertex $x = 0$ and ends at the base $x = h$. Thus, the integration boundaries are **$a = 0$** and **$b = h$**.

$$V = \pi \int_{0}^{h} y^2 \, dx = \pi \int_{0}^{h} \frac{r^2}{h^2}x^2 \, dx$$
Step 3: Integrate and substitute
$$V = \frac{\pi r^2}{h^2} \mathbf{\left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{h}}$$
$$V = \frac{\pi r^2}{h^2} \left( \frac{h^3}{3} - 0 \right)$$
$$\mathbf{V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h}$$

Volume of a Sphere

Graphical Setup:

Consider revolving the upper semi-circle centered at $(0,0)$ with radius $R$ about the $x$-axis from $x = -R$ to $x = R$.

Step 1: Write equation & isolate $y^2$

The circle centered at the origin has the formula $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$. Making $y^2$ the subject:

$$\mathbf{y^2 = R^2 - x^2}$$
Step 2: Setup integration limits

The sphere boundaries span from the leftmost point to the rightmost point on the $x$-axis. Thus, the integration boundaries are **$a = -R$** and **$b = R$**.

$$V = \pi \int_{-R}^{R} y^2 \, dx = \pi \int_{-R}^{R} (R^2 - x^2) \, dx$$
Step 3: Integrate and substitute
$$V = \pi \mathbf{\left[ R^2x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-R}^{R}}$$
$$V = \pi \left\{ \left( R^3 - \frac{R^3}{3} \right) - \left( -R^3 + \frac{R^3}{3} \right) \right\}$$
$$V = \pi \left( \frac{2}{3}R^3 + \frac{2}{3}R^3 \right)$$
$$\mathbf{V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}$$

Volume of an Ellipsoid

Graphical Setup:

Consider revolving the upper semi-ellipse centered at $(0,0)$ with semi-major axis $a$ (along $x$) and semi-minor axis $b$ (along $y$) about the $x$-axis from $x = -a$ to $x = a$.

Step 1: Write equation & isolate $y^2$

The general standard equation of an ellipse is $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$. Making $y^2$ the subject:

$$\frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2} \implies \mathbf{y^2 = b^2 \left( 1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2} \right)}$$
Step 2: Setup integration limits

Setting $y = 0$ gives the $x$-intercepts of the ellipse at $x = \pm a$. Thus, the integration boundaries are **$a_{\text{limit}} = -a$** and **$b_{\text{limit}} = a$**.

$$V = \pi \int_{-a}^{a} y^2 \, dx = \pi b^2 \int_{-a}^{a} \left( 1 - \frac{x^2}{a^2} \right) \, dx$$
Step 3: Integrate and substitute
$$V = \pi b^2 \mathbf{\left[ x - \frac{x^3}{3a^2} \right]_{-a}^{a}}$$
$$V = \pi b^2 \left\{ \left( a - \frac{a}{3} \right) - \left( -a + \frac{a}{3} \right) \right\}$$
$$V = \pi b^2 \left( \frac{2a}{3} + \frac{2a}{3} \right)$$
$$\mathbf{V = \frac{4}{3}\pi a b^2}$$
